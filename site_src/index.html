Earthquakes, cyclones, floods, droughts, fires and disaster mapping, as the issuing authorities report them.

USGS earthquakes with their PAGER impact alert, USGS volcano alerts, GDACS multi-hazard alerts, NOAA National Hurricane Center storms, Copernicus EMS rapid-mapping activations and NASA FIRMS satellite fire detections, collected every hour.

Alerts above the bottom rung

PAGER yellow or worse, GDACS orange or red, and any US volcano above Normal. PAGER and GDACS are model estimates of impact, issued within minutes and revised as reports arrive; a GDACS alert is the highest the event has reached. Volcano alerts are set by the responsible USGS observatory.

US volcanoes above Normal

The USGS volcano alert level (Normal, Advisory, Watch, Warning) speaks to people on the ground; the aviation colour code (Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) to aircraft. Outside the US, GDACS's orange and red volcano alerts appear above. The Smithsonian's daily eruption report is not used: its site refuses automated readers.

Green earthquakes are left to USGS, and green wildfires (hundreds a fortnight) are not collected.

Fire and heat detections by country, past 24 hours

VIIRS on NOAA-20, NOAA-21 and Suomi-NPP, nominal and high confidence. These are detections, not fires: a fire is counted again on each overpass, and gas flares, volcanoes and industrial heat sources are detected too. Each half-degree cell is counted to the country at its centre of detections. Radiative power (fire radiative power, in megawatts) is a rough measure of how much is burning.
