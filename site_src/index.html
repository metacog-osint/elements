Earthquakes, cyclones, weather warnings, floods, droughts, fires and disaster mapping, as the issuing authorities report them.

USGS earthquakes with their PAGER impact alert, checked against EMSC's catalogue, USGS volcano alerts, GDACS multi-hazard alerts, NOAA hurricane, weather-warning, severe-storm and tsunami products, European national weather services' warnings through MeteoAlarm, the US Drought Monitor, US wildfire incidents from NIFC, Copernicus EMS rapid-mapping activations and NASA FIRMS satellite fire detections, collected every hour.

Alerts above the bottom rung

PAGER yellow or worse, GDACS orange or red, any US volcano above Normal, and Extreme US or red European weather warnings. PAGER and GDACS are model estimates of impact, issued within minutes and revised as reports arrive; a GDACS alert is the highest the event has reached. Volcano alerts are set by the responsible USGS observatory, weather warnings by the national weather service.

US volcanoes above Normal

The USGS volcano alert level (Normal, Advisory, Watch, Warning) speaks to people on the ground; the aviation colour code (Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) to aircraft. Outside the US, GDACS's orange and red volcano alerts appear above. The Smithsonian's daily eruption report is not used: its site refuses automated readers.

US weather warnings and watches, Severe or Extreme

National Weather Service alerts rated Severe or Extreme on the alert standard's four-step scale (Minor, Moderate, Severe, Extreme). Storm-based warnings are drawn as the area NWS drew; the rest as a dot at each county they name.

Weather warnings in Europe

Official warnings from the national weather services of 39 countries (the EU, the UK, Norway, Switzerland, the Balkans, Ukraine, Moldova and Israel), gathered by MeteoAlarm. Its levels: yellow, potentially dangerous; orange, dangerous; red, very dangerous (green, no particular awareness, is left out). Warnings that start later today or tomorrow are included. Each is drawn as the polygon its service drew, or as a dot at each region it names.

Tsunami messages, past 7 days

From NOAA's National Tsunami Warning Center (Alaska; the US and Canadian coasts) and Pacific Tsunami Warning Center (Hawaii; the Pacific and Caribbean). An information statement means an earthquake was assessed and no tsunami threat is expected for the centre's coasts.

US drought this week

US Drought Monitor categories as a share of the US, including Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico. Shares are cumulative: D2 counts everything in D2, D3 or D4. Switch on "US drought" to see the map.

GDACS alerts by hazard, past 14 days

Green earthquakes are left to USGS, and green wildfires (hundreds a fortnight) are not collected.

Fire and heat detections by country, past 24 hours

VIIRS on NOAA-20, NOAA-21 and Suomi-NPP, nominal and high confidence. These are detections, not fires: a fire is counted again on each overpass, and gas flares, volcanoes and industrial heat sources are detected too. Each half-degree cell is counted to the country at its centre of detections. Radiative power (fire radiative power, in megawatts) is a rough measure of how much is burning.

US wildfires being fought

From the National Interagency Fire Center: wildfires not yet declared out that are either short of full containment or were found in the last 14 days. Prescribed burns are left out. The map shows every one; the feed and this list, those of 100 acres or more.
